Backgrounds

A Background is a Group that scrolls at its own speed when the camera moves. Far hills that slide by slowly, a sky that stays put, clouds that drift, fog in front of everything: this is parallax, and it makes a flat scene look deep.

Anything can go inside one: Sprites, Shapes, a Tiles piece, a whole scene. The pieces inside keep their own positions; the Background moves them together when the game draws them.
scrollX, scrollYHow much it moves with the scene when the camera moves. 1: like everything else. 0.5: half as fast, so it looks far away. 0: it stays on the screen. Above 1: faster, for things in front
repeatWidth, repeatHeightDraws what's inside again every this many pixels, as far as the view goes: an endless background from one picture (0: once)
driftX, driftYSlides by itself, in pixels a second: clouds, rain, a starfield
offsetX, offsetYShifts it; change them from a script to scroll by hand
left, top, right, bottomEdges: while the view is past one, it stops scrolling its own way and moves with the scene

Everything else is a Group's: layer and the order in the scene decide what's in front, tint colors it, visible hides it, and a Light or the Tint lights what's inside like any piece (turn lit off for a sky that should stay bright at night).

#Three layers

A sky, hills and a forest behind a platformer level:

  1. Add a Background called Sky and set scrollX and scrollY to 0. Put a Sprite with the sky picture inside it, placed so it fills the game's screen (its frame in the scene view).
  2. Add a Background called Hills with scrollX 0.3 (and scrollY 0.3, or 1 to keep it level with the ground). Put the hills Sprite inside, with its left edge at x = 0, and set repeatWidth to the picture's width: the hills now go on for ever.
  3. Add a Background called Forest with scrollX 0.6 and the same repeat for its own picture.
  4. Then the level itself (Tiles, the player, a Camera that follows the player), after the Backgrounds in the scene so it's drawn in front.
Level
├─ Sky       Background   scroll 0
│  └─ Sprite
├─ Hills     Background   scroll 0.3, repeatWidth 480
│  └─ Sprite
├─ Forest    Background   scroll 0.6, repeatWidth 480
│  └─ Sprite
├─ Ground    Tiles
└─ Player    Body
   └─ Camera

No code is needed. For clouds, add one more Background with a cloud picture, a repeatWidth and driftX = -10.

#Where things end up

A Background is drawn where you placed it while the camera shows the scene's first screen: the area from 0, 0 to the game's width and height, which is also what a scene without a Camera shows. So place background pictures against that frame in the scene view. When the camera moves away from there by some distance, the Background follows the scene by scroll times that distance. The scene view shows Backgrounds as placed, with a note of their scroll and an outline of the repeat size.

Zooming the camera zooms Backgrounds too, around the middle of the screen.

With a repeat size, what's inside should fit between 0 and the repeat size (measured from the Background): a 480-wide picture with its left edge at x = 0 and repeatWidth = 480 tiles without gaps. A smaller repeat makes copies overlap; a bigger one leaves gaps, which suits single clouds or lamp posts.

-- Scroll a starfield by hand instead of with the camera
function update(dt)
  Stars.offsetX -= shipSpeed * dt * 0.2
end
-- Where is it now? getScroll() gives how far it's drawn from its place
local x, y = Hills:getScroll()

#Good to know

  • Backgrounds are for looks. A Body or Zone inside one collides where it was placed, not where it's drawn, and worldPosition() gives the placed position.
  • In a repeating Background, a Light or an Occluder shines or blocks in one copy only.
  • Particles inside a Background should have followPiece on, or they are drawn where they were made in the scene.
  • A Background inside a Background adds both movements.
  • A scene without a Background pays nothing for the feature.
