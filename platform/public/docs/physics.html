Physics

Every scene has a physics world (Box2D 3). Bodies are solid, Zones notice what goes in and out, Hitboxes give either one more shapes, Joints connect bodies, and solid tiles are walls and floors. Physics runs 60 times a second, after every piece's physicsUpdate(dt).

Units: lengths in pixels, speeds in pixels per second (vx, vy), angles in radians (rotation, spin in radians per second, clockwise positive), mass in kilograms. y points down: vy = -400 goes up.

#Bodies

A Body's motion says what moves it:

motionMoved byUse it for
stillNothing, unless a script moves it (then it carries what stands on it and pushes physics bodies)Floors, walls, moving platforms
scriptedIts script: set vx, vy and call move()Players, enemies, anything you control
physicsPhysics: gravity, bumps, pushes, jointsCrates, balls, debris, ragdolls

Change motion any time: a crate can be still until it's knocked loose.

#Shapes

shape
autoFits the Sprites and Shapes inside it: a box around them, or exactly a lone circle or polygon Shape
rectanglewidth x height, centered on the Body
circleradius
capsulewidth x height with round ends: good for characters
polygonpoints {x1, y1, x2, y2, ...}, up to 8 corners (a dent is filled in)
noneOnly its Hitboxes

Shapes move, turn and scale with the piece (and the pieces it's inside). A Hitbox inside a Body or Zone adds a shape at the Hitbox's position and rotation; off = true turns it off (a sword's reach only while swinging). getSize() is the size of the box around a Body's shapes.

#Moving scripted bodies

speed, gravity = 220, 1800

function physicsUpdate(dt)
  vx = axis("left", "right") * speed
  vy = math.min(vy + gravity * dt, 1000)
  if pressed("jump") and onGround() then vy = -jumpSpeed(96) end
  move()
end

move() goes vx * dt, vy * dt and:

  • slides along walls, floors and ceilings, and takes the speed into them off vx, vy (landing sets vy to 0);
  • walks up and down slopes up to slope (45° by default) at full speed, without sliding back or hopping off going down; steeper ones are walls;
  • stays on the ground over small steps and edges going down;
  • rides what it stands on: moving platforms, physics bodies;
  • pushes physics bodies it walks into, as hard as its mass against theirs allows (a 1 kg player shoves a 1 kg crate, barely moves a 20 kg one), and presses down on what it stands on (a seesaw tips);
  • lands on one-way tiles from above and jumps up through them;
  • turns by spin * dt if spin isn't 0.
onGround()The last move ended standing on something walkable (and not going up)
onWall()The last move ran into a wall
onCeiling()The last move ran into a ceiling
moveBy(dx, dy)Goes straight until something is in the way; returns what it hit, or nil

Setting x, y or rotation moves a scripted or still body there; within 64 pixels in a step it pushes what's in the way, further it jumps there. applyImpulse(ix, iy) on a scripted body changes vx, vy by the impulse over its mass; applyForce does the same over one step.

#Physics bodies

vx, vy and spin are the body's speed now: read them, or set them to throw it. Settings take effect the next step.
applyImpulse(ix, iy, x?, y?)A sudden push: the speed changes by impulse / mass. At a point in the scene it also spins it
applyForce(fx, fy, x?, y?)A steady push for one step: call it every physicsUpdate for wind, thrusters, magnets
applyTorque(t)A steady twist for one step
applyAngularImpulse(i)A sudden twist
wake()Wakes it if it's asleep
grab(x, y), dragTo(x, y), drop()Hold it by a point with a spring, for dragging and throwing (below)
Setting
massKilograms. Impulses and forces scale with it: applyImpulse(0, -400 * mass) sends anything up at 400 px/s
gravityHow much gravity pulls it: 0 floats, -1 falls up
friction, bounceGrip (0 is ice) and bounciness (0 to 1)
drag, spinDragSlow down its movement and its turning, like air
fixedRotationNever turns: for upright characters moved by physics
bulletChecked continuously against other physics bodies too, so it can't pass through them however fast it goes. Walls and floors are always checked
asleepPhysics bodies that stop fall asleep (and cost nothing); anything touching them wakes them. Set it true to start a stack at rest

Setting x, y or rotation moves a physics body there and keeps its speed (set vx, vy, spin = 0, 0, 0 as well to stop it).

-- A bomb: everything near it flies away.
function explode()
  for _, p in ipairs(overlap("circle", x, y, 150)) do
    if p.motion == "physics" then
      local dx, dy = p.x - x, p.y - y
      local d = math.max(dist(0, 0, dx, dy), 1)
      p:applyImpulse(dx / d * 600 * p.mass, dy / d * 600 * p.mass)
    end
  end
  destroy()
end

#Collisions

hit(other, contact) runs once when a Body starts touching another body or solid tiles (and runs on both). contact has:
x, yWhere they touch
nx, nyThe direction from other towards this body (landing on a floor: 0, -1)
impulseHow hard: mass times the speed lost. Small for a touch, big for a crash
 
function hit(other, contact)
  if contact.impulse > 300 then sfx("thud", math.min(contact.impulse / 2000, 1)) end
  if other:is("spikes") then reload() end
end
isTouching(other)True if it touches a piece (a Body, Zone or Tiles) or anything with a tag
touching(tagOrPiece)The first body or zone with a tag (or that piece) touching it, or nil
contacts()What it touches now: a list of {piece, x, y, nx, ny, impulse}
touchingTile(setting)The settings of a touching tile with that setting ("damage"), or nil

Tags filter what bumps into what: a Body passes through bodies with a tag in its passThrough ("ghost, pickup"), and they pass through it. "tiles" passes through solid tiles.

#Zones

A Zone notices bodies and zones going in and out: entered(other) and exited(other) run on the Zone, and entered(zone) / exited(zone) on a Body. detects limits it to things with some tags; active = false stops it noticing; inside() lists what's in it now. Nothing bumps into a Zone, and Zones don't notice still bodies.

-- scripts/pickup.lua on a coin Zone that detects "player"
function entered(other)
  sfx("coin")
  root():emit("collected", self)
  destroy()
end

#Tiles

Tiles marked solid in their tileset are walls and floors, smooth at the seams between tiles (nothing catches on them). oneWay tiles are only solid from above: land on them, jump up through them. setTile, fill and clear change them right away. See Tiles.

#Queries

Global in scripts, and on smitten.physics:

Call
raycast(x1, y1, x2, y2, tags)The first body or solid tiles on a line: { piece, x, y, nx, ny, fraction }, or nil. tags (optional) only counts pieces with one of them.
raycastAll(x1, y1, x2, y2, tags)Everything on a line, nearest first: a list of { piece, x, y, nx, ny, fraction }.
piecesAt(x, y, tags)The bodies and zones whose shapes contain a point.
overlap(kind, x, y, ...)The bodies and zones touching a shape: overlap("circle", x, y, radius) or overlap("rect", x, y, width, height).
getGravity()The scene's gravity: x, y in pixels per second per second.
setGravity(x, y)Changes the scene's gravity: setGravity(0, 0) floats everything.
 
-- Can the enemy see the player? (Nothing solid in between.)
local hit = raycast(x, y, player.x, player.y)
local sees = hit and hit.piece == player

-- What's under the mouse?
local mx, my = mouse()
for _, p in ipairs(piecesAt(mx, my)) do print(p.name) end

Ray casts skip Zones and the backs of tiles; fraction is how far along the line the hit is (0 to 1).

#Gravity

The scenes' gravity is in Project settings › Physics (physics.gravity in smitten.json, [0, 980] by default). setGravity(0, 0) turns it off for a space level; getGravity() reads it. A body's gravity scales it for that body. Scripted bodies make their own gravity (vy += gravity * dt).

#Joints

A Joint connects body a to body b, or to the place where the Joint is when b is empty. It works where the Joint piece is: put a pin Joint on a door's hinge.

kind
pinTurns around the Joint. motor, limit (minAngle, maxAngle), spring
weldHolds them together; with spring it bends and wobbles
ropeAt most length apart (0: as far as they start)
springPulls towards length apart: stiffness (wobbles per second), damping (1: no overshoot)
distanceExactly length apart, a rod
sliderSlides along the Joint's x direction (turn the Joint to aim it). motor, limit (lower, upper), spring
wheelTurns around the Joint and springs along its y direction, like a car's wheel. motor drives it
mousePulls a's point under the Joint towards wherever the Joint moves

Settings change while the game runs (motorSpeed = 5). collide lets the two bodies still bump into each other. With breakForce above 0 the joint breaks when pulled harder and the Joint gets broke(). getAngle(), getTranslation(), getLength(), getSpeed(), getForce() and getTorque() read it.

-- A seesaw: the plank is a physics Body; this Joint sits at its middle.
-- kind = "pin", a = "Plank", b empty: pinned to the scene.
-- A motorized drawbridge:
Hinge.kind, Hinge.motor, Hinge.motorSpeed = "pin", true, -1
Hinge.limit, Hinge.minAngle, Hinge.maxAngle = true, -1.4, 0

#Dragging and throwing

grab(x, y) holds a physics body by that point with a spring; dragTo(x, y) moves the point; drop() lets go and the body keeps its speed, so a quick flick throws it. It swings and bumps into things on the way. Other bodies follow the point exactly. The ready-made Drag script (drag.lua) does this with the mouse or a finger:

function update(dt)
  local mx, my = mouse()
  if smitten.mouse.pressed(1) then
    for _, p in ipairs(piecesAt(mx, my)) do
      if p == self then grab(mx, my) end
    end
  end
  if smitten.mouse.down(1) then dragTo(mx, my) else drop() end
end

#LÖVE's love.physics

love.physics.newWorld and the rest of LÖVE's physics API work as in LÖVE (Box2D 2.4), for code that makes its own worlds. It's a separate module (lovephysics), included when a game's code uses it; scenes don't need it.

#Under the hood

The world is our fork of Box2D 3.1.1 (deps/box2d3, changes in PATCHES.md), in the physics2d module. Box2D counts 64 pixels to a meter, which only tunes its tolerances; it steps 60 times a second with 4 substeps. Still bodies are static (kinematic once moved), scripted ones kinematic and moved with Box2D's character mover, physics ones dynamic; Zones are sensors; solid tiles are chain segments, rebuilt 16 x 16 cells at a time when they change. The same project runs the same way every time.
