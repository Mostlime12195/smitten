Physics

Each scene has a physics world, which uses Box2D:

  • Bodies are solid things that hit each other.
  • Zones see when things go in and out.
  • Hitboxes give Bodies and Zones more shapes.
  • Joints connect bodies.
  • Solid tiles are walls and floors.

The engine updates the physics world 60 times per second, immediately after it calls physicsUpdate(dt) on all pieces.

Lengths are in pixels, and speeds are in pixels per second. Angles are in degrees, clockwise (spin is in degrees per second). Mass is in kilograms. As everywhere in Smitten, y points down, so vy = -400 moves up.

#Bodies

The motion of a Body sets what moves it:

motionMoved byUse it for
stillNothing, unless a script moves itFloors, walls, moving platforms
scriptedIts script, which sets vx and vy and calls move()Players and enemies
physicsThe physics world: gravity, collisions, forces, jointsCrates, balls, debris

You can change the motion at any time. For example, a crate can stay still until something hits it.

The shape of a Body is the part that collides:

shape
autoFits the Sprites and Shapes inside the Body (the default)
rectanglewidth × height
circleradius
capsulewidth × height with round ends. Good for characters
polygonUp to 8 corners in points: {x1, y1, x2, y2, ...}
noneOnly its Hitboxes

Shapes move, turn and scale with the piece. A Hitbox inside a Body or a Zone adds one more shape. Use it for shapes that auto cannot make, for example an L shape or the reach of a sword. To disable a Hitbox, set its off.

#Moving players and enemies

The script of a scripted Body moves it. Set the speed that you want. Then call move():

gravity = 1800       -- how fast it falls

function physicsUpdate(dt)
  vx = axis("left", "right") * 220
  vy = math.min(vy + gravity * dt, 1000)         -- gravity, with a maximum speed
  if pressed("jump") and onGround() then vy = -jumpSpeed(96) end
  move()
end

jumpSpeed(height) calculates the speed for a jump of that height. It uses the gravity of the script, or a gravity that you give it: jumpSpeed(96, 1200).

move() does the difficult parts of platformer movement for you:

  • It slides along walls.
  • It walks up and down slopes to a maximum of slope degrees.
  • It stays on the ground over small bumps.
  • It moves with moving platforms.
  • It pushes physics bodies. A larger mass pushes them more.
  • It jumps up through one-way tiles and lands on them.
onGround(), onWall(), onCeiling()What the last move() hit
moveBy(dx, dy)Moves in a straight line until something blocks it, and returns what it hit

When you set x, y or rotation directly, the body goes there. Over short distances, it pushes away the things in its path.

#Physics bodies

Physics bodies move by themselves. vx, vy and spin are their velocity now. To throw a body, set them. To push bodies, use these methods:
applyImpulse(ix, iy)A sudden push, like a jump or a kick. If you give a point (applyImpulse(ix, iy, x, y)), it also turns the body
applyForce(fx, fy)A constant push for one step. For wind or thrusters, call it in each physicsUpdate
applyTorque(t), applyAngularImpulse(i)The same for turning (the values are strengths of the twist, not angles)
grab(x, y), dragTo(x, y), drop()Hold the body at a point, move it, and release it (it keeps its momentum)
wake()Wake a body that is at rest
Property
massHow heavy it is, in kilograms
gravityScaleHow much gravity pulls it: 1 is usual, 0 floats, -1 falls up
friction, bounceFrom 0 to 1: grip, and bounce
drag, spinDragMake its movement and its turning slower, like air resistance
fixedRotationIt never turns
bulletMore careful collisions, for small things that move fast
asleepIt starts at rest, until something touches it

When you set x, y or rotation of a physics body, the body goes there immediately, and it keeps its speed. This explosion throws all the bodies near it away from it:

for _, p in ipairs(overlap("circle", x, y, 150)) do
  if p.motion == "physics" then
    local dx, dy = p.x - x, p.y - y
    local d = math.max(dist(0, 0, dx, dy), 1)
    p:applyImpulse(dx / d * 600 * p.mass, dy / d * 600 * p.mass)
  end
end

#Collisions

When a Body starts to touch something, the engine calls hit(other, contact) in its script. It does this for the two bodies. contact tells where they touched and the force of the hit:

contact
x, yWhere they touched
nx, nyThe direction from other to this body
impulseThe force of the hit: small for a touch, large for a crash
 
function hit(other, contact)
  if contact.impulse > 300 then sfx("thud") end
  if other:is("spikes") then reload() end
end
isTouching(pieceOrTag)Whether it touches that piece, or a piece with that tag, now
touching(pieceOrTag)The first piece that it touches and that matches, or nil
contacts()All the things that it touches: {piece, x, y, nx, ny, impulse}
touchingTile(setting)The tile properties of a touched tile that has the tile property setting

passThrough is a list of tags. The body goes through pieces with these tags, and they go through the body. For example, a ghost can go through "walls". The tag "tiles" goes through solid tiles.

#Zones

A Zone sees when bodies and other zones go in and out. It does not block anything. The engine calls entered(other) and exited(other) in its script. It also calls them in the script of the Body that went in.

  • detects limits the Zone to things with some tags.
  • active = false turns the Zone off.
  • inside() gives a list of what is in the Zone now.

Zones do not see still bodies.

#Forces in zones

A Zone can change how physics bodies move while they are inside it. Turn on its forces property. Then the Zone can give bodies its own gravity, slow them down with drag and push them with a steady force.

Property
gravityscene: bodies feel only the scene's gravity. add: the Zone's gravity adds to the scene's gravity. replace: bodies feel the Zone's gravity and not the scene's gravity
gravityX, gravityYThe Zone's gravity, in pixels per second per second. replace with 0 and 0 makes bodies float
toCenter, gravityStrengthPull towards the Zone's position, like a planet. The pull has the same strength everywhere in the Zone
drag, spinDragSlow down the movement and the turning of bodies inside. They add to the body's own drag and spinDrag
pushX, pushYA steady push on each body inside. It is a force, so a heavy body speeds up less than a light body

These rules apply:

  • Only physics bodies feel the forces. A scripted body moves only when its script moves it, and a Zone ignores still bodies.
  • A body's gravityScale multiplies the Zone's gravity, as it multiplies the scene's gravity. It does not change the push or the drag.
  • The Zone's gravity and push use the scene's directions. They do not turn when the Zone turns.
  • detects also limits who feels the forces. A Zone with active = false does nothing.
  • Zones can overlap. Gravity from add zones, drags and pushes all add up. If two or more zones replace gravity, the zone that the body went into last wins.
  • A Zone with forces off, the default, works as it always did.

Scripts can change all of these properties while the game runs. For example, Fan.forces = false turns a fan off.

#Recipes

Water. Make a Zone the size of the water. Set gravity to replace, gravityY to -150 and drag to 3. Things that fall in slow down and then float up to the top. This is not true buoyancy: a heavy crate and a light ball float the same way. To make a thing sink, set the Zone's detects to floats and tag only the floating things floats. The Zone then ignores the rest, and they fall through. Add a splash in the Zone's script:

function entered(other)
  if other.motion == "physics" and math.abs(other.vy) > 100 then sfx("splash") end
end

Wind. Set pushX to 300 and leave gravity at scene. Light things blow away, and heavy things only slide.

Conveyor belt. Make a thin Zone along the top of the belt. Set pushX to the strength of the belt. Use a negative value to go left.

Planet. Make a circle Zone around a round still Body. Set gravity to replace, turn on toCenter and set gravityStrength. Things fall onto the planet from every side. Bodies that move fast enough go into orbit.

Black hole. Use add and toCenter with a large gravityStrength, and add some drag. Things fall in and stay near the center.

Space. Use replace with gravityX and gravityY at 0. Bodies inside float and keep their speed.

#Tiles

Solid tiles in a Tiles piece are walls and floors with smooth seams, so nothing catches on the edges between tiles. One-way tiles are solid only from above. When you change tiles with setTile, fill or clear, the change has an effect immediately. See Tiles.

#Queries

These functions get data about the scene from the physics world. You can use them in all scripts, and on smitten.physics:

Call
raycast(x1, y1, x2, y2, tags)Returns the first body or solid tiles on a line: { piece, x, y, nx, ny, fraction }, or nil. If you give tags, it counts only pieces with one of them.
raycastAll(x1, y1, x2, y2, tags)Returns everything on a line, nearest first: a list of { piece, x, y, nx, ny, fraction }.
piecesAt(x, y, tags)Returns the bodies and zones whose shapes contain a point.
overlap(kind, x, y, ...)Returns the bodies and zones that touch a shape: overlap("circle", x, y, radius) or overlap("rect", x, y, width, height).
getGravity()Returns the gravity of the scene: x, y in pixels per second per second.
setGravity(x, y)Changes the gravity of the scene: setGravity(0, 0) makes everything float.
 
-- Can this enemy see the player, with nothing solid between them?
local hit = raycast(x, y, player.x, player.y)
local sees = hit and hit.piece == player

Raycasts do not see Zones, and they do not see the back sides of tiles.

#Gravity

You set the gravity of the scene in Project settings › Physics (physics.gravity, [0, 980] by default). setGravity(0, 0) turns it off, for a level in space. getGravity() gets it. Scripted bodies apply their own gravity in their script, as in the example above. A Zone can change the gravity in one part of the scene: see Forces in zones.

#Joints

A Joint connects body a to body b. When b is empty, it connects a to a point in the scene. The Joint works at the position of the Joint piece. For this reason, put the hinge Joint of a door at the hinge.

kind
pinTurns around the Joint, like a hinge or a wheel on an axle. It can have a motor, a limit (minAngle, maxAngle) and a spring
weldHolds them together. With spring, it bends
ropeKeeps them at a maximum distance of length
springPulls them to a distance of length, with stiffness and damping
distanceKeeps them at exactly the distance length, like a rod
sliderSlides along the x direction of the Joint, like a piston. It can have a motor, a limit (lower, upper) and a spring
wheelTurns, with a spring along the y direction of the Joint, like the wheel of a car
mousePulls a to the position of the Joint

You can change the properties of a Joint while the game runs (motorSpeed = 5). collide lets the two connected bodies continue to hit each other. When a Joint has a breakForce, it breaks when a pull is stronger than that force. Then it sends the broke event. getAngle(), getTranslation(), getLength(), getSpeed(), getForce() and getTorque() get its state.
