Physics

Every scene has a physics world, powered by Box2D. Bodies are solid things that bump into each other, Zones notice when things go in and out, Hitboxes give either one extra shapes, Joints connect bodies together, and solid tiles act as walls and floors. Physics updates 60 times a second, right after every piece's physicsUpdate(dt).

Lengths are in pixels, speeds in pixels per second, angles in degrees (clockwise; spin is degrees per second), and mass in kilograms. As everywhere in Smitten, y points down, so vy = -400 moves up.

#Bodies

A Body's motion decides what moves it:

motionMoved byGood for
stillNothing, unless a script moves itFloors, walls, moving platforms
scriptedIts script, by setting vx, vy and calling move()Players and enemies
physicsThe physics world: gravity, collisions, forces, jointsCrates, balls, debris

Motion can change at any time, so a crate can sit still until something knocks it loose.

A Body's shape is the part that collides:

shape
autoFits the Sprites and Shapes inside the Body (the default)
rectanglewidth × height
circleradius
capsulewidth × height with rounded ends; good for characters
polygonUp to 8 corners in points: {x1, y1, x2, y2, ...}
noneOnly its Hitboxes

Shapes move, turn and scale with the piece. A Hitbox inside a Body or Zone adds another shape, for things auto can't make, like an L shape or a sword's reach; set its off to disable it.

#Moving players and enemies

Scripted bodies are moved by their script. Set the speed you want, then call move():

gravity = 1800       -- how fast it falls

function physicsUpdate(dt)
  vx = axis("left", "right") * 220
  vy = math.min(vy + gravity * dt, 1000)         -- gravity, with a top speed
  if pressed("jump") and onGround() then vy = -jumpSpeed(96) end
  move()
end

jumpSpeed(height) works out the speed for a jump that high from the script's gravity (or give it one: jumpSpeed(96, 1200)).

move() does the hard parts of platformer movement for you: it slides along walls, walks up and down slopes up to slope degrees, stays on the ground over small bumps, rides moving platforms, pushes physics bodies (harder the heavier its mass), jumps up through one-way tiles and lands on them.
onGround(), onWall(), onCeiling()What the last move() ran into
moveBy(dx, dy)Moves in a straight line until blocked, and returns what it hit

Setting x, y or rotation directly moves a body there, pushing aside what's in the way over short distances.

#Physics bodies

Physics bodies move on their own. vx, vy and spin are their current velocity; set them to throw something. To push them around:
applyImpulse(ix, iy)A sudden push, like a jump or a kick. Applied at a point (applyImpulse(ix, iy, x, y)), it also spins the body
applyForce(fx, fy)A steady push for one step; call it every physicsUpdate for wind or thrusters
applyTorque(t), applyAngularImpulse(i)The same for turning (twisting strengths, not angles)
grab(x, y), dragTo(x, y), drop()Hold the body by a point, move it, and let go (it keeps its momentum)
wake()Wake a body that has come to rest
Setting
massHow heavy it is, in kilograms
gravityScaleHow strongly gravity pulls it: 1 is normal, 0 floats, -1 falls up
friction, bounceFrom 0 to 1: grip, and bounciness
drag, spinDragSlow its movement and its turning, like air resistance
fixedRotationNever turns over
bulletExtra-careful collisions for small, fast things
asleepStart resting until something touches it

Setting x, y or rotation of a physics body teleports it and keeps its speed. Here's an explosion that throws everything nearby outwards:

for _, p in ipairs(overlap("circle", x, y, 150)) do
  if p.motion == "physics" then
    local dx, dy = p.x - x, p.y - y
    local d = math.max(dist(0, 0, dx, dy), 1)
    p:applyImpulse(dx / d * 600 * p.mass, dy / d * 600 * p.mass)
  end
end

#Collisions

When a Body starts touching something, its script's hit(other, contact) runs, on both bodies. contact says where and how hard:

contact
x, yWhere they touched
nx, nyThe direction from other towards this body
impulseHow hard they hit: small for a touch, large for a crash
 
function hit(other, contact)
  if contact.impulse > 300 then sfx("thud") end
  if other:is("spikes") then reload() end
end
isTouching(pieceOrTag)Whether it's touching that piece, or anything with that tag, right now
touching(pieceOrTag)The first matching piece it's touching, or nil
contacts()Everything it's touching: {piece, x, y, nx, ny, impulse}
touchingTile(setting)The settings of a touched tile with that setting

passThrough lists tags a body goes straight through, both ways: a ghost might pass through "walls". "tiles" passes through solid tiles.

#Zones

A Zone notices bodies and other zones going in and out, without blocking anything. Its script gets entered(other) and exited(other), and so does the Body that went in. detects limits it to things with certain tags, active = false turns it off, and inside() lists what's in it right now. Zones don't notice still bodies.

#Tiles

Solid tiles in a Tiles piece are walls and floors with smooth seams, so nothing catches on the edges between tiles. One-way tiles are solid only from above. Changing tiles with setTile, fill or clear takes effect immediately. See Tiles.

#Queries

These ask the physics world about the scene. They work in any script, and on smitten.physics:

Call
raycast(x1, y1, x2, y2, tags)Returns the first body or solid tiles on a line: { piece, x, y, nx, ny, fraction }, or nil. If you give tags, it counts only pieces with one of them.
raycastAll(x1, y1, x2, y2, tags)Returns everything on a line, nearest first: a list of { piece, x, y, nx, ny, fraction }.
piecesAt(x, y, tags)Returns the bodies and zones whose shapes contain a point.
overlap(kind, x, y, ...)Returns the bodies and zones that touch a shape: overlap("circle", x, y, radius) or overlap("rect", x, y, width, height).
getGravity()Returns the gravity of the scene: x, y in pixels per second per second.
setGravity(x, y)Changes the gravity of the scene: setGravity(0, 0) makes everything float.
 
-- Can this enemy see the player, with nothing solid in between?
local hit = raycast(x, y, player.x, player.y)
local sees = hit and hit.piece == player

Raycasts skip Zones and the backs of tiles.

#Gravity

The scene's gravity is set in Project settings › Physics (physics.gravity, [0, 980] by default). setGravity(0, 0) switches it off for a space level, and getGravity() reads it. Scripted bodies apply their own gravity in their script, as in the example above.

#Joints

A Joint connects body a to body b, or to its own spot in the scene when b is empty. It acts where the Joint piece is, so a door's hinge Joint goes at the hinge.

kind
pinTurns around the Joint, like a hinge or a wheel on an axle. Can have a motor, a limit (minAngle, maxAngle) and a spring
weldHolds them together; with spring, it flexes
ropeKeeps them at most length apart
springPulls them towards length apart, with stiffness and damping
distanceKeeps them exactly length apart, like a rod
sliderSlides along the Joint's x direction, like a piston. Can have a motor, a limit (lower, upper) and a spring
wheelTurns, with a spring along the Joint's y direction, like a car's wheel
mousePulls a towards wherever the Joint is

Joint settings can change while the game runs (motorSpeed = 5). collide lets the two connected bodies still bump into each other. With a breakForce, the joint snaps when pulled harder than that and sends broke. getAngle(), getTranslation(), getLength(), getSpeed(), getForce() and getTorque() read its state.
