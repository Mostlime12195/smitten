Camera

A Camera sets which part of the scene is on the screen. If a scene has no Camera, the screen shows the scene with (0, 0) at the top-left corner. UI pieces stay in the same position on the screen, when the camera moves and when it does not.

#Following the player

The simplest method is to put the Camera inside the player. Then the Camera moves with the player. A different method is to set the target of the Camera to the player in the Inspector, or to call Camera:follow(player). With this method, the Camera can be in any position in the scene, and it can change to a different target later.

  • smoothing makes the Camera move smoothly after its target. 0 makes the Camera stay exactly on the target. 5 makes it move gently.
  • offsetX and offsetY move the view. For example, use them to show more of the level in front of a player who runs.
  • After you teleport the player, call snap(). The camera then goes to the player immediately.
-- On the player: show more of the level in the direction that the player runs
function update(dt)
  Camera.offsetX = approach(Camera.offsetX, sign(vx) * 60, 120 * dt)
end

#Keeping it inside the level

left, top, right and bottom are limits. The camera does not show the scene past these edges.

  • setLimits(left, top, right, bottom) sets all four limits.
  • limitTo(level) uses the painted area of a Tiles piece as the limits. Because of this, the view does not show empty space outside the level.

If the area in the limits is smaller than the screen, the camera puts the area in the center.

#Zoom and shake

zoom = 2 makes all things two times bigger. To zoom smoothly, tween zoom. shake(strength, seconds) shakes the view, and the shake becomes weaker until it stops. For example, use shake(6, 0.3) for a hit and shake(16, 0.8) for an explosion.

#Several cameras

Smitten uses the first active camera in the scene. makeCurrent() changes to a different camera. camera() gives the camera that Smitten uses now.

#Screen and scene positions

Positions on the screen are in game pixels. Positions in the scene are in the units of the scene, through the camera. These functions change one type of position to the other:
mouse()The position of the pointer in the scene
Camera:screenToWorld(x, y)A point on the screen, as a point in the scene
Camera:worldToScreen(x, y)A point in the scene, as a point on the screen
Camera:getView()The part of the scene that is on the screen: x, y, width, height
 
-- Spawn a bat immediately past the right edge of the screen
local vx, vy, vw, vh = camera():getView()
spawn("scenes/bat.scene", { x = vx + vw + 32, y = vy + rnd(vh) })

For layers that scroll slower or faster than the camera (for example, far hills, or a sky that does not move), see Backgrounds.
