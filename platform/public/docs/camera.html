Camera

A Camera decides which part of the scene is on the screen. Without one, the screen shows the scene from (0, 0) at the top-left corner. UI pieces stay put on the screen whatever the camera does.

#Following the player

The simplest way is to put the Camera inside the player: it moves with them. Or set the Camera's target to the player in the Inspector (or call Camera:follow(player)), which lets the camera live anywhere and switch targets later.

smoothing makes it glide after its target instead of sticking to it (0 sticks, 5 is gentle). offsetX and offsetY shift the view, for example to look ahead of a running player. After teleporting the player, snap() jumps the camera straight there.

-- On the player: look ahead in the direction it's running
function update(dt)
  Camera.offsetX = approach(Camera.offsetX, sign(vx) * 60, 120 * dt)
end

#Keeping it inside the level

left, top, right and bottom stop the camera from showing anything past those edges. setLimits(left, top, right, bottom) sets all four, and limitTo(level) uses the painted area of a Tiles piece, so the view never shows empty space outside the level. If the limited area is smaller than the screen, it's centered.

#Zoom and shake

zoom = 2 makes everything twice as big; tween it for a smooth zoom. shake(strength, seconds) shakes the view and fades out: shake(6, 0.3) for a hit, shake(16, 0.8) for an explosion.

#Several cameras

The first active camera in the scene is the one used. makeCurrent() switches to another camera, and camera() returns the one in use.

#Screen and scene positions

The screen is measured in game pixels, and the scene in its own units through the camera. These convert between them:
mouse()The pointer's position in the scene
Camera:screenToWorld(x, y)A point on the screen, as a point in the scene
Camera:worldToScreen(x, y)A point in the scene, as a point on the screen
Camera:getView()The part of the scene on screen: x, y, width, height
 
-- Spawn a bat just past the right edge of the screen
local vx, vy, vw, vh = camera():getView()
spawn("scenes/bat.scene", { x = vx + vw + 32, y = vy + rnd(vh) })

For layers that scroll slower or faster than the camera (far hills, a sky that stays put), see Backgrounds.

PropertyTypeDefault
activebooltrueWhen this is on, the game uses this camera. If more than one camera is active, the first in the scene has priority.
zoomnumber1The zoom: 2 shows everything twice as big.
smoothingnumber0How gently the camera catches up: 0 follows exactly, 5 glides.
targetpiecepath""A piece to follow, anywhere in the scene. If it is empty, the camera stays at its own position.
offsetXnumber (px)0The camera looks this far to the right of what it follows.
offsetYnumber (px)0The camera looks this far below what it follows.
leftnumber (px)noneThe camera does not show anything to the left of this edge. If it is empty, there is no edge.
topnumber (px)noneThe camera does not show anything above this edge.
rightnumber (px)noneThe camera does not show anything to the right of this edge.
bottomnumber (px)noneThe camera does not show anything below this edge.
Method
shake(strength?, seconds?)Shakes the view: shake(6, 0.3).
follow(piece)Follows a piece. With nil, the camera goes back to target, or to its own position.
snap()Moves directly to what it follows, without smoothing (for example, after a teleport).
setLimits(left, top, right, bottom)Sets the edges that the camera does not show past (nil for no edge).
limitTo(piece)Keeps the view inside the painted part of a Tiles piece.
makeCurrent()Makes this the camera in use and turns the other cameras off.
getView()Returns the part of the scene that is on the screen: x, y, width, height.
screenToWorld(sx, sy)Takes a point on the screen and returns it as a point in the scene.
worldToScreen(x, y)Takes a point in the scene and returns it as a point on the screen.
