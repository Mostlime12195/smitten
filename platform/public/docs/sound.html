Sound and music

Smitten plays .wav, .ogg, .mp3 and .flac files. It also plays the sound effects (.sfx) and songs (.song) that you make in the Sound and Music workspaces of the editor. There are two methods to play them:

  • from a script, with sfx and music.
  • with a Sound piece in a scene.
sfx("coin")                                -- finds coin.sfx (or coin.wav...) in all the project
sfx("hit", { volume = 0.5, pitch = 0.9 + rnd(0.2) })
music("theme")                             -- plays again and again, also after the scene changes
music("boss", { fade = 1 })                -- fades from the old music to this music
stopMusic(2)                               -- fades out in 2 seconds
Call
sfx(name, volume, pitch)Plays a sound once. sfx("coin") finds coin.sfx (or .wav, .ogg...) anywhere in the project. You can also give options: sfx("hit", { volume = 0.5, pitch = 1.2 }).
music(name, options)Plays music in a loop, in place of the current music: music("theme", { volume = 0.6, fade = 1 }).
stopMusic(fade)Stops the music. If you give fade, the music fades out over fade seconds.
pauseMusic()Pauses the music.
resumeMusic()Continues the music.
masterVolume(volume)Sets the volume of every sound, 0 to 1. masterVolume() returns the current value.

#Finding sounds

If you give only a name, for example "coin", Smitten finds the sound with that name in all of your project. If more than one file has the name, Smitten uses the .sfx file. A full path, for example "sounds/coin.wav", plays only that file.

Some random pitch (pitch = 0.9 + rnd(0.2)) makes sounds that play many times sound more natural, for example footsteps or hits. A pitch of 2 is one octave higher.

#Volume and pitch

Each call of sfx can have its own volume and pitch. Give them as numbers, or in an options table:

sfx("ouch", 0.8)                                       -- volume 0.8
sfx("ouch", 0.8, 1.2)                                  -- volume 0.8, pitch 1.2
sfx("ouch", { pitch = 0.9 + rnd(0.3), volume = 0.8 })  -- options
  • volume is from 0 to 1. The default is 1.
  • pitch is 1 for the normal pitch. 2 is one octave higher and two times faster. 0.5 is one octave lower and two times slower. The default is 1. If the pitch is 0 or less, the sound plays at a pitch of 0.01.
  • Volume and pitch work for all sound files and for .sfx files.
  • If a value is not a number, sfx stops with an error that tells you which value is wrong.

This example plays a recorded "ouch" each time that something hurts the player. Each "ouch" has a different pitch. Because of this, the sound does not become boring.

-- scripts/player.lua
hp = 3

function hurt(amount)
  hp -= amount
  sfx("ouch", { pitch = 0.9 + rnd(0.3), volume = 0.8 })
end

#Record a sound

You can record a sound with your microphone in the editor. The editor saves the recording as a WAV file in your project.

  1. Open the Sound workspace.
  2. Click Record in the list at the left.
  3. If the browser asks, let it use the microphone.
  4. Make the sound. The waveform shows the sound while you record.
  5. Click Stop, or press Space.

You can also start a recording in two other ways:

  • Click + at the right of the tabs. Then click Record a sound.
  • In the Files panel, click New. Then click Recording.

After you stop, the editor puts the start marker and the end marker around the loud part of the recording. The editor saves the recording as sounds/recording.wav. Rename the file in the Files panel, for example to ouch.wav. Then sfx("ouch") plays it.

  • A recording can be 10 seconds long at most. At 10 seconds, the recording stops.
  • The editor saves recordings as mono WAV files at 22050 Hz. A recording of 10 seconds is 441 KB. The engine keeps sound files of less than 512 KB in memory. Because of this, a recording can play many times at the same time.

If the recording does not start, the editor shows a message:

  • If the browser blocks the microphone, allow the microphone for the site in the address bar of the browser. Then click Record again.
  • The browser lets only secure pages use the microphone: pages that start with https://, and localhost. If you open the editor with http:// from a different computer, the Record button is off. Open the editor at its https:// address.

#Change a sound file

The sound file editor opens all the .wav files of the project, for example a file that you imported. To open a file, click it in the Files panel.

  • Trim: drag the start marker and the end marker on the waveform. You can also drag across the waveform to select a part. The arrow keys move the selected marker by 0.01 seconds (with Shift, by 0.1 seconds). Fit to the sound puts the markers around the loud part. Use all selects all of the sound.
  • Volume: 100% is the sound as it is. Normalize makes the loudest part as loud as possible, but not louder than full volume. If the volume is too high, the parts that clip show in red on the waveform.
  • Speed: 2× makes the sound two times shorter and one octave higher. 0.5× makes the sound two times longer and one octave lower.
  • To play the result, click the waveform or press Space.

The editor saves the result to the file automatically. While the file is open, the editor keeps all of the original sound. Because of this, you can make a trim wider again, or undo a change (Ctrl+Z). When you close the tab, the parts that you trimmed are not in the file any more.

The editor saves WAV files with 16 bits for each sample. An imported file keeps its sample rate and its channels (mono or stereo).

This example makes a short "bap" for a drum:

  1. Record yourself when you say "bap".
  2. Click Fit to the sound.
  3. Drag the end marker to the left until the sound is short.
  4. Click Normalize.
  5. Rename sounds/recording.wav to sounds/bap.wav.

#Music

music(name) plays a sound again and again as background music. It replaces the music that played before. If you ask for the music that plays now, nothing changes. Because of this, each level can safely call music("theme"). Music continues when the scene changes. Pausing the game does not stop the music, and timeScale does not make it slower.

#Sample instruments

In a song, an instrument can play a sound file of your project, for example a recording. This is a sample instrument. Each note plays the sound at the pitch of the note. A recorded "bap" can be a drum, and a recorded "la" can sing a melody.

  1. Record the sound (see Record a sound), or import a sound file.
  2. Open the song in the Music workspace.
  3. Select a channel.
  4. Open Edit instrument.
  5. Click Sound file.
  6. Choose the file in the list.
  7. Set Base note. A note on this row plays the sound as it is.
  8. Draw notes on the rows. Each row higher plays the sound faster and one semitone higher.

The instrument has these controls:

Control
Base noteThe note that plays the sound as it is.
PitchedWhen this is off, every note plays the sound as it is. Use this for drums.
Play the whole soundWhen this is on, each note plays all of the sound. When this is off, the sound stops at the end of the note, after the release.
Volume, Attack, Decay, Sustain, ReleaseThe volume and the envelope, as for synth instruments.
Vibrato depth, Vibrato speed, Low-passAs for synth instruments.

In the .song file, a sample instrument looks like this example. It is a recorded "la" with the base note G4 (67):

{ "name": "La", "type": "sample", "sample": "../sounds/la.wav", "baseNote": 67, "playToEnd": false, "release": 0.2 }

A note with "note": 72 (C5) is 5 semitones higher than the base note. Because of this, it plays la.wav 1.33 times faster. The note stops at the end of its length, and then fades out in 0.2 seconds.

Songs with sample instruments play in the game as they play in the editor. The engine makes the song from the same samples. For OGG and MP3 files, the preview in the editor can sound a little different from the game, because the browser reads these files.

#The Sound piece

A Sound piece plays one file. You choose the file in the Inspector. A Sound piece is good for these sounds:

  • sounds that belong to a thing in the level (for example, a waterfall or the hum of an engine).
  • sounds that must start with the scene (autoplay).
  • sounds that a script starts, stops and changes in detail.

Its volume and pitch can change while it plays. Because of this, you can tween them. The Sound piece stops when it goes out of the scene.

function ready() fadeIn(2) end   -- an alarm that becomes louder
PropertyTypeDefault
soundresource""The sound file.
autoplayboolfalseWhen this is on, the sound plays when the scene starts.
loopboolfalseWhen this is on, the sound starts again at the end.
volumenumber1The loudness, from 0 to 1.
pitchnumber11 is normal, 2 is one octave higher.
Method
play()Plays the sound and returns its Source (see smitten.audio). If you call it while the sound plays, another copy starts (not for .song files).
stop()Stops every copy.
pause()Pauses the sound.
resume()Continues the sound after pause().
isPlaying()Returns true while the sound plays.
fadeIn(seconds, volume?)Plays the sound from silence up to its volume property (or to the volume that you give) over some seconds.
fadeOut(seconds)Makes the sound quieter until it is silent, then stops it. Then the volume property goes back to its old value.
seek(seconds)Moves to a time in the sound.
tell()Returns the current play position, in seconds.
getDuration()Returns the length of the sound, in seconds.
Event (a function in its script)
function finished()The engine calls it when the sound reaches its end (not when it loops).

#smitten.audio

Function
smitten.audio.sfx(name, volume?, pitch?)Plays a sound once by name or path: smitten.audio.sfx("coin", { volume = 0.5 }).
smitten.audio.music(name, options?)Plays music in a loop, in place of the current music. options: volume, pitch, fade.
smitten.audio.stopMusic(fade?)Stops the music. If you give fade, the music fades out over fade seconds.
smitten.audio.pauseMusic()Pauses the music.
smitten.audio.resumeMusic()Continues the music.
smitten.audio.getMusic()Returns the Source that plays the music, or nil.

smitten.synth.sfx(path) and smitten.synth.song(path) load .sfx and .song files. They give objects that you can play and stop in your script (File formats).
