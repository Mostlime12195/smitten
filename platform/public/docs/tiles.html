Tiles

A Tiles piece is a grid of cells that you paint with tiles from a tileset. A tileset is a picture with a grid of squares, and each square is one tile. Each tile can have tile properties. For example:

  • solid: the tile blocks bodies.
  • oneWay: the tile is solid only from above.
  • A tile property of your own, such as damage.

You paint the tiles in the scene view. Scripts can read and change the cells while the game runs: for blocks that break, for doors, or for levels that a script makes.

Cell numbers are columns and rows from the position of the Tiles piece. The top-left cell is (0, 0), and negative numbers are also correct. Each cell holds a tile number or -1. Tile numbers start at 0 and count across the tileset picture. The number -1 means that the cell is empty.

#Reading and changing cells
getTile(column, row), setTile(column, row, tile)Get or change one cell. The tile -1 empties the cell
fill(column, row, columns, rows, tile)Fill a rectangle of cells
clear()Empty all cells
cellsWith(tile)All painted cells (or all cells with that tile), as {column, row, tile}
neighbors(column, row, diagonal)The cells next to one cell, as {column, row, tile}
tileInfo(tile)The tile properties of a tile: solid, name, damage...
isSolid(column, row)Whether the tile in that cell is solid

When you change a cell, a solid tile in it blocks bodies immediately.

#Cells and positions
cellAt(x, y)The cell at a point in the scene
tileAt(x, y)The tile at a point, or -1
cellPosition(column, row), cellCenter(column, row)The corner or the center of a cell, in the scene
getTileSize()The size of one cell in the scene
getBounds()The painted area: x, y, width, height

You can paint marker tiles in the Tiles piece and change them into pieces when the level starts:

-- Change each coin tile (tile 7) into a coin piece
function ready()
  for _, c in ipairs(Level:cellsWith(7)) do
    local cx, cy = Level:cellCenter(c.column, c.row)
    spawn("scenes/coin.scene", { x = cx, y = cy })
    Level:setTile(c.column, c.row, -1)
  end
end

#Finding paths through tiles

newPathGrid() makes a grid that knows which cells are solid. Use it to find the way through a maze of tiles. The grid follows the changes that setTile(), fill() and clear() make. Pathfinding tells you more about grids.

local grid = Level:newPathGrid()
local path = grid:findPoints(x, y, Player.x, Player.y)   -- {x, y, x, y, ...} or nil

#Touching tiles

When a Body hits solid tiles, the engine calls its hit(other) with the Tiles piece as other. touchingTile(setting) gives the tile properties of a touched tile that has the tile property setting. This is good for hazards:

local t = touchingTile("damage")
if t then hurt(t.damage) end

When castShadows is on, the solid tiles block the Lights that have shadows on.

#Terrains

A terrain is a set of tiles that show one material in all its shapes. The shapes are the tile that is alone, the edges, the corners and the middle. When you paint with a terrain, each cell gets the tile that fits the cells next to it. A platform gets its ends and a lake gets its shore, and you do not pick the tiles yourself. A tileset can have many terrains (grass, stone, water). A tile is part of one terrain only.

To make a terrain, select a Tiles piece. Then open Terrains at the side of the Tiles panel, and do these steps:

  1. Click New terrain.
  2. Give the terrain a name.
  3. Mark the tiles of the terrain. When Mark its tiles is on, each tile in the picture has nine parts:
    1. Click the middle of each tile of the terrain.
    2. Click the sides and corners where that tile joins more of the same terrain.

    For example, if the grass in a tile goes to the right side of the tile, mark the right side. For a tile in the middle of an area of grass, mark all the parts.

  4. Click Paint with it.
  5. Paint the terrain in the scene view. The brush, the rectangle, the bucket and the eraser fit the edges of the cells that they change. They also fit the edges of the cells around those cells. One stroke is one undo step, as with all painting.

To paint with plain tiles again, set Paint with to Picked tiles, or pick a tile.

If your picture has one of the usual layouts (below), choose the layout in Fill in from a layout. This marks the full sheet in one step. Before you do this, pick the top-left tile of the sheet. You can also mark the tiles of a different layout by hand.

Neighbours that count sets which cells around a cell change its tile:

A cell looks atDifferent tiles
Sides and cornersIts four sides. It also looks at a corner when the two sides next to that corner are the same terrain47 (a "blob" set)
SidesIts four sides16
CornersEach corner. A corner counts when the three cells around that corner are the same terrain16 (a 2 × 2 or "wang corner" set)

If two or more tiles have the same marks, they are variants. Each cell picks one variant from its position. Because of this, a cell always shows the same variant, and the tiles do not flicker. A set that does not have all its tiles also works. In that case, a cell gets the nearest tile that the set has. A wrong side counts more than a wrong corner.

#Terrains in scripts
setTerrain(column, row, terrain)Paints a terrain (by name) in a cell, and fits the 8 cells around it. nil erases
fillTerrain(column, row, columns, rows, terrain)The same for a rectangle of cells
getTerrain(column, row)The name of the terrain in a cell, or nil
terrains()The names of the terrains of the tileset
 
-- Make a hole where the player clicks. The grass around it gets new edges.
function update(dt)
  if pressed("mouse:1") then
    local column, row = Level:cellAt(mouse())
    Level:setTerrain(column, row, nil)
  end
end
-- A random cave. All its walls fit.
function ready()
  Level:fillTerrain(0, 0, 60, 40, "rock")
  for i = 1, 400 do
    Level:setTerrain(math.random(1, 58), math.random(1, 38), nil)
  end
end

setTile is exact: it puts the tile that you give in the cell, and it does not change the cells next to it. Cells hold only tile numbers. The terrain of a cell comes from its tile. Because of this, maps do not need new data, and you can add terrains to a tileset later.

#Animated tiles

A tile can be an animation: it shows other tiles in turn. Use this for water, lava, torches or conveyor belts. To make an animated tile:

  1. In the Tiles panel, open Animation.
  2. Drag across the animation frames in the picture, from the first to the last.
  3. Click Animate with these tiles.

You paint with the first tile. The tile properties of the first tile (solid, damage...) are the ones that count. The picture shows the animation.
FramesThe tiles that it shows, in sequence
Frames a secondThe speed, when all animation frames have the same time
Seconds for each frameA different time for each animation frame, for example 0.8, 0.1, 0.1
Random startEach cell starts at a different point of the loop, so that a lake does not move in one wave

Animated tiles use the clock of the game: they stop while the game pauses, and they go slower with timeScale(). The animate property of the Tiles piece stops all of them: Level.animate = false stops them on the animation frame that they show. Between changes of animation frame, they use no time, for any number of animated tiles.

#Slopes

Tiles are square: a solid tile blocks its full cell. For a ramp or a hill, do these steps:

  1. Paint the tiles of the slope without solid.
  2. Put a still Body over them.
  3. In the Body, put a polygon Shape with the outline of the slope.

Bodies that move with move() go up slopes that are not steeper than their slope property (Physics).
