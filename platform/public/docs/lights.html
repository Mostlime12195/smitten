Lights

Three pieces make light and darkness, in the Light and shadow group of Add a piece:

  • A Light shines on the pieces around it.
  • An Occluder blocks light, so lights can throw shadows.
  • A Tint colors the whole scene: dark blue for night, orange for sunset.

They are building blocks, and each works without the others:

In the sceneWhat you get
A TintA color grade: everything multiplied by the Tint's color
LightsThe scene as usual, brighter where the lights shine
A dark Tint and LightsNight, with pools of light
Lights with shadows on, and OccludersShadows behind the Occluders

The rule behind it: a piece is shown in its own colors multiplied by the light that falls on it. That light starts as the Tint's color (white if there's no Tint), and each Light adds to it where it shines.

The on-screen UI (Labels, Buttons, Bars...) is never lit or tinted.

#A night scene with a torch

  1. Add a Tint. Its color starts as a dim blue, so the scene turns to night straight away. Make it darker for a cave, lighter for dusk.
  2. Add a Light inside the player, so it moves with them. Set radius to how far the torch reaches and color to a warm orange.
  3. Turn on the Light's shadows.
  4. Put an Occluder inside each thing that should block the light, next to its Sprite or Shape: a crate, a pillar, a wall. Its shape starts as auto, which fits that Sprite or Shape, the way a Body does. For walls painted with a Tiles piece, turn on the Tiles piece's castShadows instead: every tile marked solid in the tileset blocks light.

That's all of it, with no code. To make the torch flicker, give the Light a script:

function update(dt)
  brightness = 0.9 + rnd(0.2)
end

and to light it up or put it out smoothly:

tween(Torch, 0.5, { brightness = 0 })

#Lights

A Light's kind is point or sun.

  • A point light shines out from where the piece is, as far as radius, fading towards the edge. Give it an image to change its shape: the light is the picture, white where it shines, stretched so it is radius wide each side of the piece. A cone-shaped picture makes a flashlight (turn the Light to aim it), a window-shaped one makes light through a window. Scaling the Light stretches it.
  • A sun lights the whole view evenly. It only has a direction, the way the piece is turned (rotation 0 shines to the right, 90 down), which is where its shadows point. shadowLength keeps a sun's shadows short, which suits games seen from above.

brightness is how strong the light is and color its color. off turns it off; a hidden Light (visible off, or inside a hidden piece) is off too.

blend is what the light does to the light already there:

blend
addMakes it brighter. Lights add up.
subtractMakes it darker: a patch of gloom, a blob shadow under a character
mixReplaces it with this light's color: a colored spotlight that looks the same by day and by night

Everything is an ordinary property, so scripts change them and tween animates them:

Lamp.color = { 1, 0.2, 0.2 }           -- alarm!
tween(Lamp, 0.3, { radius = 400 })

#Shadows

A Light with shadows on is blocked by every Occluder, and by the solid tiles of Tiles pieces with castShadows on.

An Occluder's shape is auto, rectangle, circle or polygon. auto fits the Sprites and Shapes inside the Occluder; when it has none, it fits the ones in the piece it is inside. So both of these work:

Crate (Body)              Pillar (Occluder)
  Look (Sprite)             Look (Sprite)
  Shadow (Occluder)

The shadow starts at the far side of the Occluder, so the thing that blocks the light is lit itself. off lets light through again (a door opening).

On the Light:

  • shadowColor is what is left of the light inside a shadow. Black (the start) leaves none. Lower its opacity for fainter shadows.
  • shadowSoftness blurs the shadows' edges by that many pixels. 0 is sharp and quickest.
  • shadowsFrom is a list of tags: only Occluders and Tiles with one of them block this light (the tag can be on the Occluder or on the piece it is inside). Empty means all of them.

#Which pieces a light lights

  • lit is a setting every piece with a position has. Turn it off and the piece, and everything inside it, ignores Lights and the Tint: it always shows at full brightness. Use it for things that glow by themselves: a laser, lava, a sign, a firefly. It keeps its place in the drawing order.
  • A Light's lights is a list of tags: it only lights pieces with one of them, and what's inside those pieces. lights = "player" makes a light that picks out the player and leaves the floor dark.
  • A Light's fromLayer and toLayer limit it to pieces on those layers. A Tint has the same two settings: toLayer = 0 on a Tint leaves everything on layer 1 and above untinted.
-- Lightning: a sun that lights only the background layers, for a moment
Flash.fromLayer, Flash.toLayer = -10, -1
Flash.brightness = 3
tween(Flash, 0.4, { brightness = 0 })

#Day and night

A Tint's color is a list of three numbers, and tween can change them:

-- On the scene's root, with a Tint called Sky and a sun Light called Sun
local day = { 1, 1, 1 }
local dusk = { 1, 0.6, 0.45 }
local night = { 0.2, 0.25, 0.45 }

function ready()
  while true do
    tween(Sky.color, 8, dusk)
    tween(Sun, 8, { brightness = 0.2, rotation = 180 })
    wait(8)
    tween(Sky.color, 8, night)
    tween(Sun, 8, { brightness = 0 })
    wait(20)
    tween(Sky.color, 8, day)
    Sun.rotation = 0
    tween(Sun, 8, { brightness = 0.5 })
    wait(20)
  end
end

Lamps that come on at night are Lights whose brightness you tween up when it gets dark; windows that glow are pieces with lit off.

The first Tint in the scene that isn't off is the one used, like Cameras.

#Try it

tools/scene-tests/lights in Smitten's source is a small project to open in the editor: scenes/demo.scene is a night scene with a torch on the mouse, a lamp, crates and a wall of tiles that throw shadows, and a glowing sign. (scenes/main.scene is the engine's own test of all of this.)

#How fast it is, and its limits

A scene without a Light or a Tint is drawn exactly as before: none of this costs anything until you use it.

  • Each Light is one picture drawn over the area it reaches. Dozens of small lights are fine on a school laptop; what costs is area, so a few huge lights cost more than many small ones. Lights that are off, hidden, or don't reach the view cost nothing.
  • Shadows cost more: for each Light with shadows on, the far edges of the Occluders within its reach are drawn. Turn shadows on for the lights where they matter (the player's torch), not for every candle. shadowSoftness above 0 adds two blur passes per light.
  • Occluder outlines are worked out again only when the Occluder moves or changes. Tiles merge runs of solid tiles into single edges.
  • Lights that use lights tags or a layer range are drawn into their own light map, one for each different combination of lights in view. Up to 8 combinations and 24 such lights are handled at once; past that a warning is printed and some pieces get the wrong lights.
  • Shadows are hard-edged shapes behind each Occluder; shadowSoftness blurs them by the same amount everywhere, rather than more further away.
  • A sun's endless shadows use shadow-casting tiles up to two screens away from the view; set shadowLength for exact, shorter shadows.
  • Lights blend in the order they joined the scene, which only matters for mix.
  • A script's draw() is lit like the piece, unless it sets a shader of its own.
